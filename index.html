About Me

Research at the intersection of atmospheric composition, radiative forcing, and climate response.

I am a PhD Candidate in Atmospheric & Oceanic Sciences at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru, where I have been working in CAOS since February 2021. My research focuses on quantifying and understanding the climate impacts of key anthropogenic emissions and comparing how different radiative forcings translate into climate responses.

My work uses the radiative kernel technique together with controlled experiments in the Community Earth System Model / Community Atmosphere Model framework. I am particularly interested in methane, black carbon aerosol, stratospheric sulfate aerosol injection, and solar-radiation-modification strategies, with an emphasis on diagnosing feedbacks rather than considering radiative forcing in isolation.

Published · Journal of Climate · 2025

Contrast in slow climate response to methane and carbon dioxide forcings identified using the radiative kernel technique

This study compares the slow climate responses to CO₂ and CH₄ radiative forcing using CAM5 experiments and radiative kernels. For comparable radiative forcing, the efficacy of the 10×CH₄ experiment is lower than that of 1.35×CO₂. The difference is associated with a more negative aggregate feedback response for methane, with important contributions from lapse-rate, water-vapor, shortwave-cloud, and surface-albedo feedbacks.

Under Review · Journal of Climate · 2026

Radiative Kernel Analysis Reveals Weaker Climate Sensitivity to Black Carbon Forcing than to Carbon Dioxide

This work quantifies climate response to enhanced black-carbon forcing and compares it with a CO₂ doubling experiment in CAM5. The analysis indicates weaker warming per unit effective radiative forcing for black carbon, with the difference linked to cloud, albedo, and lapse-rate feedback changes associated with the spatially asymmetric and vertically concentrated heating from BC.

→ Manuscript submitted June 2026

Manuscript in Preparation

Contrast in Solar and Sulfate Aerosol Forcings Does Not Translate into Their Climate Responses

I designed and ran multi-decadal CESM1.2.2 experiments with CAM5 physics and the Bulk Aerosol Model to investigate volcanic sulfate aerosol injection at multiple stratospheric altitudes under a doubled-CO₂ background. The experiments use both slab-ocean and prescribed-SST configurations to compare sulfate-aerosol and solar-constant-reduction solar-radiation-modification strategies.
